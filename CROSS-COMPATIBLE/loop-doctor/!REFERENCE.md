# Loop Doctor — Reference and Citations

Primary literature supporting the use of structured psychological and behavioral methods
with machine systems. These sources motivate the level of analysis; they do not authorize
individual diagnoses, prove human equivalence, or replace local evidence.

## Machine behaviour

**Rahwan, I., Cebrian, M., Obradovich, N., et al.** (2019). “Machine behaviour.”
*Nature, 568*, 477–486. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1138-y>

Defines machine behaviour as an interdisciplinary empirical field for studying intelligent
machines as a class of actors with behavioral patterns and ecologies. It argues that
benchmark-oriented engineering is not enough to characterize how systems behave across
environments, interactions, and social contexts.

Loop Doctor applies that research level operationally: it observes recurring behavior in
the deployed agent, including how context and interaction shape the pattern.

## Using cognitive psychology to understand GPT-3

**Binz, M., & Schulz, E.** (2023). “Using cognitive psychology to understand GPT-3.”
*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 120*(6), e2218523120.
<https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2218523120>

Applies canonical cognitive-psychology experiments to GPT-3 and shows that they reveal both
competencies and failure patterns that task scores alone do not expose. The experiments are
designed to distinguish how a task is solved, not only whether it is solved.

Loop Doctor uses the same distinction: a successful output can coexist with a recurring
behavioral failure, so diagnosis depends on the pattern and its conditions rather than a
single performance result.

## Machine Psychology

**Hagendorff, T., Dasgupta, I., Binz, M., Chan, S.C.Y., Lampinen, A., Wang, J.X., Akata, Z.,
& Schulz, E.** `arXiv:2303.13988` — submitted 2023-03-24, v6 2024-08-08.
<https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13988>

Argues for running behavioral experiments drawn from psychology on large language models,
yielding "computational insights that move us toward a better understanding and discovery
of emergent abilities and behavioral patterns" — explicitly *beyond performance benchmarks*.

This is the same methodological bet Loop Doctor makes: structured behavioral observation
surfaces things benchmarks don't.

The paper’s experimental-design guidance also supports Loop Doctor’s emphasis on recurring
observable patterns rather than one-off impressions.

*Cite the v6 title, "Machine Psychology." Search results commonly return the longer v1
title, which is superseded.*

## Is Machine Psychology here?

**Löhn, L., Kiehne, N., Ljapunov, A., & Balke, W.-T.** (2024). “Is Machine Psychology
here? On Requirements for Using Human Psychological Tests on Large Language Models.”
In *Proceedings of the 17th International Natural Language Generation Conference*, 230–242.
<https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.inlg-main.19>

Reviews 25 machine-psychology studies and identifies recurring problems in reliability,
construct validity, test contamination, and reproducibility. It calls for psychological
constructs and testing procedures to be defined for large language models rather than
copied unchanged from human assessment.

Loop Doctor follows that direction by defining machine-observed patterns in operational
terms: observable signals, likely reinforcing conditions, interventions, and return checks.

## AI agent behavioral science

**Chen, L., Zhang, Y., Feng, J., et al.** (2026). “AI agent behavioral science.”
*Humanities and Social Sciences Communications, 13*.
<https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-07316-7>

Argues that agent behavior emerges from the integration of models into situated systems,
where goals, memory, environmental conditions, social cues, and interaction feedback shape
behavior over time. It proposes systematic observation, hypothesis-driven intervention,
and theory-guided interpretation across individual, multi-agent, and human-agent settings.

This is Loop Doctor’s direct level of analysis: the behavior of an agent in context across
time, rather than the base model considered in isolation.
